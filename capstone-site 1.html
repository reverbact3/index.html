The problem

Food insecurity isn't rare, and it isn't shrinking

In the United States, a meaningful share of households already go without reliable access to enough food. Around the world, the number is far larger. Neither figure is trending down.

13% of U.S. households experienced food insecurity — about 18.3 million people
5.4% of households had very low food security — roughly 7.2 million people
828M people worldwide experience undernourishment — about one in ten

The idea

A grocery-price aggregator, paired with a meal planner

Most stores don't publish prices in any easy-to-compare way, and nobody has time to check five apps before deciding what to cook. So we're building both halves: the data, and the interface that makes it useful.

Roadmap

We're scoping this in order, not all at once

Core functionality first, extra features once that's solid, polish after that. It keeps the project manageable inside a capstone timeline.

PHASE 1

Working aggregator + usable interface

Price data from five-plus stores, and a search/meal-planning screen simple enough to actually use.

PHASE 2

Additional features

Broader store coverage, better meal suggestions, saved lists.

PHASE 3

Quality-of-life polish

The refinements that make it feel finished, once the core is proven.
